# What four tools write that we cannot read

Measured with `build-oracle-corpus.sh`, which has each tool write every format it can, keeps only the
files its own author reads back, and puts the result through this registry.

## Why the coverage report needed this

The gap report beside this was generated by diffing extension lists, and that method answers a
narrower question than it appears to. Two things went wrong with it.

**It never asked ffmpeg anything.** RECOIL, XnView and ImageMagick were compared; ffmpeg was not
mentioned, and it decodes a set of image formats none of the other three cover the same way.

**Names are not extensions.** A diff of tool format-names against our extensions reports 87 missing
from ImageMagick and 10 from ffmpeg. Almost none of those is real. ImageMagick calls JPEG XL `jxl`
and ffmpeg calls it `jpegxl`; ffmpeg calls Sun Raster `sunrast` where the files are `.ras`. Thirteen
of ImageMagick's are video containers, twenty-odd are synthetic sources that generate a picture
rather than store one (`canvas`, `gradient`, `plasma`, `label`), and a dozen more are aliases of
formats already read here (`png8`, `png24`, `bmp3`, `pjpeg`). Counting those as coverage gaps
overstates the problem and buries the real ones.

Writing files and reading them back finds the real ones. It is the same method that found six
defects in one pass before, and it found nine more here.

## Result

| tool | written and re-read by its author | we read | we do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| ImageMagick 7.1.2 | 132 | 119 | 13 |
| ffmpeg n9.0 | 27 | 25 | 2 |
| XnView nconvert 7.300 | 39 | 30 | 9 |
| RECOIL 6.4.5 | writes nothing | — | — |

RECOIL's catalogue was checked by extension instead, it having no encoder: 476 of its 477 are read
here. The one that is not is `gr10p`, which RECOIL comments out of its own list for having a
five-character extension.

## The defects, worst first

  - **DPX crashes.** ffmpeg's DPX output makes the reader throw `IndexOutOfRangeException` rather
    than refuse the file. A crash is worse than a refusal: it escapes as an exception nobody
    downstream expects, and this is the only reader in the corpus that does it.
  - **`.iff` is read as a palette index when it holds colours.** *(This was first written down here
    as "dispatches to Maya only", which was wrong and is corrected: six formats claim `.iff` and the
    registry asks all six.)* What nconvert writes is a `FORM ILBM` of 24 planes with no CMAP — a
    truecolour picture, each group of eight planes one colour byte. The ILBM reader ran every plane
    count through the chunky conversion, folding 24 planes onto the lowest eight, so the red channel
    came back as a palette index and the other two were dropped. It then reported success, and
    drawing the result threw for want of a palette.
  - **`.raw` was claimed only by the camera reader**, which wants a TIFF byte-order mark. XnView
    files `raw`, `gry` and `grey` on one row against one reader, and the other two names were already
    held here.
  - **GIMP brush refuses three bytes a pixel.** The reader takes 1 and 4; nconvert writes an RGB
    brush.
  - **Psion MBM refuses UID2 `0x10000042`**, accepting only `0x10000000`.
  - **MIFF refuses nconvert's MIFF**, on the signature.
  - **Radiance under `.rad`** is refused for want of `#?`, which suggests a flavour of the format
    rather than a wrong reader.
  - **Softimage `.pic`**: nconvert's `ray` output carries `36 31 20 33` where `0x5380F634` is wanted,
    so `.pic` is at least two formats and the name alone does not settle which.
  - **`.flt` is ambiguous, and the claim here is right.** It is held for the Windows bitmap, on
    XnView's BIAS FringeProcessor row, and a DIB renamed `.flt` decodes here identically to
    nconvert's own reading of it. nconvert *also* writes ESRI float grids under the name — not
    through `-out flt`, which is not a format name, but through `-out arcib`, which emits one
    `.flt` a band with a `.hdr` beside it stating `ncols`, `nrows` and the byte order, and a payload
    of exactly `ncols * nrows` little-endian floats.
    <br>It cannot read one back: handed its own `.flt`, it answers "Don't know how to read this
    picture". So this is the `.qtl` and `.wrl` situation again — a format the tool writes and
    cannot open — and it is left rather than closed, because it is a single-band elevation grid
    per file rather than a picture, and the three bands only become one by being named alike.
    Implementable from the sidecar alone if it is ever wanted.

## Not defects, recorded so they are not re-investigated

  - `.jxl` is claimed and read as far as its header and no further. Known, and in the README.
  - `.yuv` refuses a size it cannot infer. Correct: the format states no size and guessing one draws
    a picture that is not there.
  - `.pcl` refuses a job whose palette comes from commands this reader does not read. Honest.
  - ImageMagick's `mpc`/`cache` pair and `strimg` are its own scratch formats, and `msvg`/`rsvg` are
    two names for its SVG renderers over the same file.
  - Video containers — `flv`, `mpeg`, `mpg`, `m2v`, `webm`, `wmv` — are out of scope for a still
    image library, and are listed here only so the number 13 above adds up.

## Genuinely missing formats

  - `svgz`, which is SVG under gzip and nothing else.
  - `pcds`, Photo CD's stacked form, beside the `.pcd` already read.
  - `gr10p`, RECOIL's one.
