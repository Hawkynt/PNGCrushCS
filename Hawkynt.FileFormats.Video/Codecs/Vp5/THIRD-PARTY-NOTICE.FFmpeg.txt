The On2 VP5 decoder in this directory is converted from FFmpeg (https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg):

  libavcodec/vp5.c       frame header, vector and coefficient models, coefficient parsing
  libavcodec/vp5data.h   VP5's own probability-update and context-remapping tables
  libavcodec/vp56.c      macroblock types and their statistics, motion-vector prediction, the
                         direct-current predictor, motion compensation and the reconstruction loop
  libavcodec/vp56data.c  the tables VP5 shares with VP6
  libavcodec/vp5dsp.c    the prediction-time edge filter
  libavcodec/vp3dsp.c    the 8x8 integer inverse DCT, in both the intra and the inter form
  libavcodec/vpx_rac.h   the binary arithmetic decoder and its normalisation

Why a conversion and not a specification. On2 published a VP6 bitstream specification and never
published a VP5 one, and no reverse-engineer's description covers more of VP5 than its range coder
and frame header. FFmpeg's decoder is therefore not a convenient second opinion about VP5 — it is the
only complete account of the format that exists, and every VP5 file in circulation has been checked
against it and against nothing else. Reimplementing from a description that does not exist was not an
option, so this is the first rung of the repository's sourcing ladder taken deliberately.

What is not taken from there. The zigzag scan and the range coder's normalisation table are built here
rather than transcribed, because both have definitions and a second rendering of a derivable table is
a second chance to get it wrong. The colour conversion is this package's own and deliberately differs
from FFmpeg's, which is why the oracle test compares sample planes rather than RGB. The fixed tables
were transcribed mechanically, by a script that parsed the C initialisers.
